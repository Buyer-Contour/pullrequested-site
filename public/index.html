01 / Research

Working notes

How a go-to-market agent should gather evidence, choose a test, and hand off its work.

Note 01 Market mapping

A list is not a market.

Account lists tend to flatten important differences. A better starting point is a hypothesis about who is likely to buy, what changed for them, and what might make them act now. The useful output is not just a shortlist, but a record of why each account made it onto the list.

Note 02 Outbound experiments

Design for an informative no.

A test is more useful when it can change your mind. Keep the audience and message narrow enough that a reply or a silence can tell you what to investigate next. A lack of replies is not evidence of a lack of demand until you check whether the right people saw the message.

Note 03 Agent handoffs

Pass the evidence with the answer.

The next person or agent should be able to inspect the sources, separate observations from guesses, and pick up where the last one stopped. A handoff that includes uncertainty is more useful than a polished answer that hides it.
